Formal verification can provide particularly strong evidence in support of certification claims. However, using formal verification results effectively in a certification context can be challenging, particularly when their scope, assumptions, and relevance to broader system-level claims need to be understood and communicated. This presentation identifies some of these challenges and examines what is needed for formal verification results to become compelling certification evidence.

This presentation explores how structured assurance arguments can provide a framework for addressing these challenges. Such arguments are increasingly used for complex systems, and in some industries are becoming an expected part of the certification package. These arguments can make explicit how formal verification contributes to higher-level claims, how it relates to other forms of evidence, and how together these elements support confidence in the overall safety case.
